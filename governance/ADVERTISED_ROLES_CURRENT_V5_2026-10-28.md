MANDATORY ASSESSMENT SEQUENCE


Follow this order for every vacancy. Do not score first and explain away a fundamental mismatch afterwards.


STEP 1 - VERIFY THE VACANCY


- Confirm that the vacancy is live on the employer's official careers site whenever possible.
- Record the original posting date or state clearly when it is not displayed.
- Confirm the location, work model and whether employment from Sweden is genuinely allowed.
- Confirm the actual commercial territory or market scope: local Sweden, Nordic, European, EMEA, global or another defined remit. Do not confuse being able to work from Sweden with having an international sales remit.
- Identify company headquarters/ownership where relevant to assessing the likely sales operating model. Do not reject by nationality alone.
- Use the direct employer application link where available.
- Check the tracker and previous conversations to prevent duplicate recommendations or applications.
- Do not present an old, closed, filled or unverifiable listing as a new opportunity.


STEP 2 - EXTRACT THE ACTUAL REQUIREMENTS


Separate the advert into:


A. Confirmed mandatory requirements: wording such as must, required, essential or an equivalent repeated central requirement.
B. Preferred requirements: helpful but not stated as essential.
C. Core day-to-day work: what the person will actually spend time doing.
D. Unknowns: matters the advert does not establish and which must not be invented.


Never treat a preferred qualification as mandatory without evidence. Equally, do not ignore a specialist capability merely because the advert uses softer wording when it is clearly central throughout the responsibilities.


STEP 3 - APPLY THE TWO HARD NO GATES


For fit assessment, there are only TWO automatic HARD NO conditions:


1. SWEDISH HARD NO
Withhold the role as Tier C / DO NOT APPLY only when the advert explicitly requires Swedish at a fluent, professional, native, business-fluent or equivalent working level that Andrew cannot truthfully claim.


Do NOT reject merely because Swedish is advantageous, preferred, useful, mentioned as a plus, or because the company is Swedish. If the wording is ambiguous, investigate it. If Swedish is not explicitly required, it is not a hard NO.


2. OFFICE-BASED-ONLY HARD NO
Withhold the role as Tier C / DO NOT APPLY when the advert explicitly requires office-based-only/on-site-only working with no hybrid or remote flexibility. Andrew is not seeking roles that require permanent full-time office attendance.


Do NOT turn a location preference, office location, travel expectation, hybrid pattern, customer-site work, or occasional office attendance into an office-based-only hard NO unless the advert clearly establishes that the role is office-based only.


ADMINISTRATIVE EXCLUSION - NOT A FIT GATE
A vacancy already applied to, a confirmed duplicate of an earlier application, or a role no longer live must not be surfaced for repeat application. This is an administrative exclusion, not a judgment of fit.


ALL OTHER GAPS ARE ASSESSMENT FACTORS, NOT AUTOMATIC REJECTIONS
The following may reduce Tier/score or be highlighted as shortlist risks, but must NOT automatically withhold an otherwise strong role:
- direct specialist-sector experience;
- an existing buyer/customer/partner network;
- deep ecosystem knowledge;
- people-management scope;
- P&L or organisational-leadership elements;
- account-management/customer-success elements;
- technical or engineering credentials;
- junior/high-volume or quota-heavy characteristics;
- travel technology or destination-content provenance;
- fashion/clothing provenance;
- sales-machine, CRM, quota, forecast or activity-monitoring signals;
- commercial-scale evidence;
- imperfect title fit;
- an internally inconsistent advert;
- contradictory location wording;
- unclear reporting lines;
- preferred qualifications Andrew cannot yet evidence.


When the core role itself is a strong match to Andrew's hands-on full-cycle new-business method, the default bias is to KEEP THE ROLE IN PLAY, investigate uncertainties, state the risks clearly, and recommend applying where the upside justifies it. Andrew explicitly prefers taking a shot at a strong role over withholding it because the advert is imperfect or because another candidate may have a more exact background.


If an advert is internally inconsistent but the role otherwise fits strongly, do not discard it. Flag the inconsistency and treat it as a reason to apply early and clarify during the process, unless one of the two hard NO gates above is explicitly triggered.


STEP 4 - ASSESS BRIDGEABLE GAPS


A gap may be bridgeable when it concerns a product category, customer type or sales tool that Andrew could reasonably learn and the employer has not made prior direct experience a selection condition.


Examples of credible transfer evidence include:


- learning and selling different premium products and B2B technology propositions;
- personally demonstrating SaaS to C-level executives and Heads of Legal;
- establishing proactive outbound programmes;
- building international markets and distribution;
- conducting consultative discovery, proposals, negotiation, contracts and close.


Do not manufacture sector expertise or claim that general sales experience automatically replaces mandatory domain experience.


One meaningful but genuinely bridgeable gap normally limits a role to Tier B. Several strong preferred requirements that Andrew cannot evidence usually make the role Tier C, because together they are likely to dominate shortlisting even if none is individually labelled mandatory.


SHORTLISTING RISK TEST FOR TIER B


Do not use "bridgeable" to mean merely that Andrew could learn the product after joining. The relevant question is whether the missing evidence is likely to prevent him reaching interview stage against candidates who already possess it.


For every proposed Tier B role, explicitly separate:


- learnable product knowledge;
- a different but transferable customer type;
- missing evidence that is likely to be a first-screen differentiator, such as exact enterprise scale, specialist buyer relationships, a named technology ecosystem, a sector network, or directly comparable deal history.


Use the Tier B range deliberately rather than treating 70-84 as one undifferentiated category:


- Tier B 80-84 - HIGH B: genuinely close to Tier A. The gap is clearly non-mandatory, is unlikely to dominate first-screen selection, and Andrew's transferable evidence is unusually strong.
- Tier B 75-79 - MID B: a worthwhile application, but another candidate with the exact enterprise, sector, customer or commercial-scale evidence may have a meaningful paper-screen advantage.
- Tier B 70-74 - STRETCH B: the role still passes every hard gate and the core work fits, but the paper match is materially weaker. Apply selectively where the role, company or upside justifies the stretch.


Do not inflate a role into the 80s merely because the day-to-day sales mechanics fit. The score must reflect realistic shortlist probability against the employer's stated selection criteria. If the advert makes the missing experience mandatory or central, it is not Tier B; apply the hard gate and withhold the role.


For senior enterprise SaaS or technology roles, inspect whether the employer is likely to screen for commercial scale as well as sales method. Where relevant, check for evidence such as annual quota/target level, typical or largest deal value, ACV/ARR, sales-cycle length, enterprise customer scale, territory revenue, or pipeline created. Absence of these figures is not itself a rejection gate, but it must be flagged when the advert uses explicit scale thresholds or when candidates with directly evidenced scale are likely to have a material paper advantage. Never invent numbers. If a missing metric is likely to matter and can potentially be evidenced, ask Andrew for a defensible figure before final CV tailoring.


STEP 5 - ASSESS SALES OPERATING MODEL, QUOTA AND PRESSURE CULTURE


This is now a MATERIAL PREFERENCE/RISK assessment, NOT an automatic rejection gate.


Andrew accepts revenue accountability, sensible targets, CRM use and normal pipeline visibility. A pressure-heavy or tightly monitored sales model may make a role less attractive and should reduce the fit score where evidenced, but it must not automatically convert an otherwise strong role to Tier C.


Investigate and report warning signals such as:
- intrusive CRM/activity tracking;
- weekly or relentless pipeline/forecast/commit cadence;
- repeated emphasis on individual quota attainment or over-attainment;
- explicit call/email/meeting/demo activity KPIs;
- rigid territory execution with close managerial control;
- high-volume prospecting expectations;
- rankings, aggressive pace or continual performance inspection.


Treat the overall pattern as a working-environment risk. Distinguish evidence from inference. If the role is otherwise a strong fit and neither Swedish nor office-based-only applies, keep it in consideration and let Andrew decide whether the operating model is acceptable.


Positive indicators remain genuine ownership of market-building, long-cycle strategic selling, freedom to choose accounts and methods, trust-based management, cross-border scope, and evaluation primarily on sustainable commercial outcomes.


STEP 6 - ASSIGN THE TIER


TIER A - STRONG MATCH


- Neither of the two hard NO gates applies.
- The core work strongly uses Andrew's proven full-cycle new-business strengths: prospecting, discovery, solution presentation/demonstration, proposals, negotiation and closing.
- Direct new-business ownership is central or materially important.
- Any sector, network, technical, management, scale or operating-model gaps are judged bridgeable, uncertain, secondary, or outweighed by the strength of the core match.
- An internally inconsistent advert does not prevent Tier A when the role itself is otherwise an excellent match.


TIER B - WORTH APPLYING / CONSIDERING


- Neither hard NO gate applies.
- The core commercial work fits, but there is one or more meaningful paper-screen disadvantages, role-shape compromises or working-environment concerns.
- Tier B remains actionable. A strong High B or Mid B role should normally be surfaced rather than withheld.


TIER C - LOW FIT / DO NOT PRIORITISE


- One of the two hard NO gates applies; OR
- the role is genuinely weak on Andrew's core full-cycle new-business work even after considering transferable evidence.


Do not use Tier C merely because the advert asks for specialist experience, a network, management exposure, technical familiarity, quota evidence, or another background advantage. Those are ranking factors unless Swedish or office-based-only is explicitly required.






FIT SCORE


Only calculate a fit score after the role passes the hard rejection gates. The Tier decision comes first; the score communicates strength within that decision and must never override it.


Score out of 100:


- Core full-cycle and new-business work: 0-35
- Match to evidenced experience and seniority: 0-25
- International scope and commercial autonomy: 0-15
- Product, company and mission fit: 0-10
- Language, location and work-model fit: 0-10
- Role structure and likely working environment: 0-5


Sales operating model is now scored as a material working-environment preference/risk, not an automatic Tier C gate. Strong pressure-culture evidence should reduce the score and be reported clearly, but it does not by itself override a strong core-role match.


For international scope, score the actual selling remit rather than the employee's physical location. A Sweden-only territory should receive low international-scope credit; Nordic, EMEA, European or global responsibilities should receive progressively more credit where genuinely evidenced.


Score ranges:


- 85-100: Tier A
- 80-84: Tier B - HIGH B, genuinely close to Tier A
- 75-79: Tier B - MID B, worthwhile but with a meaningful first-screen disadvantage
- 70-74: Tier B - STRETCH B, passes the gates but should be applied to selectively
- Below 70: Tier C


Do not automatically cap a role below Tier A merely because there is one meaningful sector, network, technical, scale or management gap. Score the complete match realistically. Only the two hard NO gates - explicit Swedish requirement or office-based-only working - automatically force Tier C / DO NOT APPLY.

REJECTION LEARNING AND RECORDING


For every advertised application, preserve enough information to test future patterns:


- Original Tier and fit score
- Role family and industry
- Confirmed mandatory requirements
- Bridgeable gaps and main risk identified before applying
- Application date
- Employer's exact stated outcome and reason
- Any likely explanation inferred from the advert, explicitly labelled unconfirmed
- Confidence in that inference: low, medium or high


GENERIC REJECTION LANGUAGE - MANDATORY INTERPRETATION RULE


Treat wording such as the following as generic unless the employer identifies a specific requirement, skill, credential, experience area or interview concern:


- "other candidates more closely matched our requirements";
- "other candidates' skills and experience more closely align";
- "we decided to pursue other candidates";
- "there is a gap between our requirements and your current skills and experience" without naming that gap;
- "we will not be moving forward";
- equivalent stock wording from an ATS or recruitment platform.


For these messages, record the employer outcome as a rejection and record the reason as "generic rejection - no specific reason supplied" or equivalent factual wording. Do not translate generic wording into a claimed deficiency in sales ability, seniority, CV quality, sector knowledge, language, network or any other factor.


If the role had a known pre-application gap, that gap may be recorded separately as an UNCONFIRMED POSSIBLE CONTRIBUTOR, with low/medium/high confidence and a short evidence-based rationale. Never present that inference as the employer's reason.


A rapid rejection can be recorded as timing context, but it is not proof of an automated decision, ATS filtering or any particular reason. Similar stock wording from several employers is not multiple independent pieces of evidence for the same candidate weakness.


Only specific employer feedback, explicit screening-question failure, a confirmed mandatory mismatch, or a clearly evidenced repeated pattern should materially change future governance. Human interview feedback has greater diagnostic weight than template rejection wording.


Do not count a role that was filled, withdrawn or closed as evidence against Andrew's suitability. Keep those outcomes separate from employer rejections.


Periodically audit a meaningful sample of recent advertised applications by comparing original Tier/score, known pre-application gaps, actual outcome and whether the employer supplied specific feedback. Use that evidence to recalibrate future Tiering. Do not infer an industry-wide or CV-wide problem from only one or two roles, and do not recalibrate from generic templates alone.




FINAL DECISION PRINCIPLE


The right question is not "Could Andrew possibly do this job?"


The right questions are:


1. "Is Andrew likely to be shortlisted because the employer's real selection criteria match evidence he can demonstrate now?"
2. "Does the likely day-to-day sales operating model fit the kind of autonomous, strategic, consultative new-business environment Andrew actually wants to work in?"


If either answer is no, withhold the role even when the title, company, product, salary, mission or task-level sales mechanics are attractive.
