Your confusion is fair: a large company often can do this itself. The reason to use ZenDev is usually speed, capacity, skills, flexibility or cost — not because the client has no tech team.

Simple answer

The client keeps its own tech leaders and core team. ZenDev adds extra people / a specialist team / a whole project team when the client does not want to hire everything itself.

Why use ZenDev if you already have developers?

1. Team is already full

  • Internal developers are busy with core systems.
  • New project needs to start now.
  • ZenDev runs work in parallel.
Example: keep core team on daily business; ZenDev builds the new app.

2. Hiring is too slow

  • Recruiting 10–20 senior engineers can take months.
  • ZenDev says it can show vetted people within 48h and onboard in 2–3 weeks.
Company claim — but the logic is clear: buy speed.

3. Need skills only for a while

  • Specialist mobile / integration / AI / legacy work.
  • May not justify permanent hires.
  • Bring skills in, finish work, reduce team later.

4. Flexible capacity

  • Need 5 people now, maybe 15 later.
  • Permanent hiring is harder to reverse.
  • External team can scale up/down more easily.

5. Cost

  • Bosnia has lower employment cost than Sweden.
  • ZenDev handles payroll, HR, equipment etc.
  • ZenDev claims ~40–50% lower total cost vs hiring a senior developer in Sweden.
Their claim, not independently verified here.

6. Build a foreign tech hub

  • ICA wants a 100+ person team in Sarajevo.
  • Doing that alone means local recruitment, contracts, payroll, HR, office, management setup.
  • ZenDev already has that infrastructure.
This is probably the clearest reason ICA needs a partner.

Important: they do NOT necessarily replace the in-house team

ZenDev says about half its engagements work alongside existing in-house teams. Their people join the client’s tools, stand-ups and tech leadership.
Client keeps ownership. Code, repo, docs and infrastructure remain with the client, so work can later move back in-house.
Easy way to think about it: ICA may keep strategy, architecture, product ownership and core technology leadership internally — while ZenDev builds and runs a large delivery team in Bosnia. Public material does not tell us the exact ICA split, so ask Nikola how it works in practice.
My view: ZenDev has a decent future if it moves up the value chain. The risky version is simply selling developer headcount.
